02 / 16 · Problem
The problem

Students don't need another chatbot.
They need a tutor.

Every AI "tutor" today is one of three things — and none of them is a tutor. Photomath and Mathway are answer-engines: scan, solve, move on.[7] Quizlet, Brainly, Course Hero, Chegg are chat bolt-ons on top of static study libraries.[5] MagicSchool and SchoolAI are back-office productivity tools for teachers — lesson plans and rubrics, not in-the-moment learning for the student.[8]

The result: students get faster answers, but they don't get better at thinking. Parents pay for tools they suspect their kids are using to cheat. Schools buy "AI" platforms that adults use and students ignore.

What students get today

A calculator that talks. Type a problem, get an answer, no thinking required.

A chatbot in a sidebar. The student writes problems in a tiny text box; the AI has no idea what's on their paper.

A search engine for solutions. Find a worked example someone else did. Copy it. Forget it tomorrow.

04 / 16 · Insight
The insight

The product students would actually use.

Talk to a hundred students about what a tutor would have to do to be useful. Four answers come back, every time.

1

Watch me work — don't just answer my question.

A real whiteboard with a tutor reading every stroke. The "what part are you stuck on?" question that great human tutors ask becomes obvious when the AI can see the page.

2

Talk to me — typing isn't how I think out loud.

Voice in, voice out, with an animated tutor face. ChatGPT's Advanced Voice Mode reset the floor — students now expect to talk to AI the way they'd talk to a person.[14]

3

Push me — don't solve it for me.

Strict Socratic mode: the tutor refuses to reveal the final answer until the student has shown a step. It's pedagogy. It's also the defence against "this is a cheating tool."[13]

4

Work with what I have — handwriting, photo, voice, any device.

Snap a textbook problem. Write the equation by hand. Talk to it. Switch from laptop to iPad. Multimodal isn't a feature — it's the table-stakes baseline.[12]

The product · shipping today

Four capabilities competitors can't combine.

✎

Infinite whiteboard, built for learning

Pen, eraser, text, shapes (rect, circle, line, arrow, triangles, polygons, 3D solids), smart drawing that auto-cleans rough sketches, multi-page tabs, pan and zoom — a real canvas, not a notes field.

⌨

Handwriting + camera input

Write equations by hand or snap a textbook problem with your phone camera. Claude vision reads the canvas, returns structured math, and routes it straight into the tutor dialogue.[12]

♪

Voice tutor with animated avatar

Press-to-talk in. Tutor speaks back through the Web Speech API. An animated avatar blinks, thinks, and speaks in sync — the conversation loop closes without a keyboard.

★

Strict Socratic mode

One toggle. Tutor will not reveal the final answer until the student writes a step on the canvas. Pedagogical guardrail and the PR shield against academic-integrity backlash.[13]

The technical moat

Canvas, ink OCR, voice avatar UX, and pedagogy-tuned prompting all have to be good simultaneously. A chat company would need to rebuild their product. A whiteboard company would need to learn pedagogy.

The data flywheel

Every session generates the only training corpus that exists for "student stroke sequence → understanding state." This corpus only spins for whoever owns the canvas — and unlocks the next moat: confusion detection.

The pedagogy positioning

Strict Socratic is both a feature and a sales weapon. Schools and parents are buying "AI tutoring without cheating" — and most products structurally can't claim it.[13]

Go-to-market

Three motions, sequenced.

Consumer first to prove product-love. Schools second to prove revenue durability. Partners third to scale beyond direct distribution.

Motion 1 · 0-12 mo

Consumer (B2C)

Channel: TikTok student creators, parent Facebook groups, Discord study servers, organic SEO ("Photomath alternative").

Goal: 5,000 paying families by end of year 1. Free-to-paid conversion benchmark: 4-6%.

Motion 2 · 6-18 mo

Schools (B2B)

Wedge: Teacher live-dashboard for in-class use — not back-office. Land via 3 design-partner districts in year 1.

Goal: 30 paid schools by end of year 2. Avg ACV $15K. Procurement-friendly: Clever / Google SSO from day one.

Motion 3 · 18+ mo

Partners (B2B2C)

Play: Embeddable SDK — "Stripe for AI tutoring." Test-prep brands (Magoosh, PrepScholar), language apps, Khan Academy district partners.
